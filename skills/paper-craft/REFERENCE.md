# Paper Craft Reference

Read only the section matching the detected document type, plus section 6 for citations and section 7 for the self-check detail.

## 1. Research article, IMRaD

Order is fixed: Title, Abstract, Keywords, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion, References.

- **Title:** concise, specific, keyword-rich. Name population or phenomenon, key variables, and design when possible.
- **Introduction, funnel:** broad context, relevant prior work, explicit gap, then one prominent objective or hypothesis at the end. Focused, not a full review.
- **Methods, replicable:** design and justification, setting and participants with inclusion and exclusion criteria, materials with versions and sources, chronological procedures, analytic plan with missing-data handling, ethics approval and consent, plus reporting guideline: CONSORT for trials, STROBE for observational studies.
- **Results, data only:** follow the order of the aims. Tables and figures carry detail, text highlights patterns. Report effect sizes, confidence intervals, and exact p values or credible intervals. Do not interpret.
- **Discussion:** link each interpretation to a specific result. Compare with prior studies, explain divergences, acknowledge limitations, state theoretical and practical implications, suggest next research. Balanced tone.
- **Conclusion, 1-3 paragraphs:** answer the question, restate the takeaway, note relevance. No new data.
- **Abstract recipe:** 1-2 sentences background and objective, brief design and sample, principal findings with magnitude, takeaway and relevance.

## 2. Systematic review, PRISMA

- **Question:** stated as PICO or equivalent, with registered protocol when the venue expects it.
- **Methods:** databases, search strings, dates, inclusion and exclusion criteria, screening and extraction process, risk-of-bias assessment.
- **Flow:** PRISMA diagram counts at identification, screening, eligibility, inclusion.
- **Results:** study characteristics table, bias table, synthesis of findings with heterogeneity noted.
- **Discussion:** strength of evidence, practice implications, limitations of evidence and of the review, future directions.
- **Supplements:** full search strings and excluded-with-reason list when required.

## 3. Philosophy essay, thesis-objection-reply

- **Thesis:** one precise claim, scoped to what the length can defend.
- **Terms:** define key terms once, use one meaning throughout.
- **Reasons:** one argument per paragraph, ideally in P1/P2/C form. Exposition first, evaluation after.
- **Objections:** strongest rival versions stated charitably, one per paragraph.
- **Replies:** answer each objection or concede and narrow the thesis.
- **Road markers:** announce moves. Example: "I give two arguments, then answer two objections."
- **Intro and conclusion:** short intro ending in the thesis, conclusion restating what the debate looks like now. Minimal headings.
- **Voice:** formal analytic. First person allowed for the thesis, no filler opinion phrases.

## 4. Humanities paper, stakes-review-thesis

- **Stakes paragraph:** text under study, question addressed, why it matters beyond the text.
- **Literature review:** how prior scholarship frames the problem and where the gap sits. Chronological when the topic is historical.
- **Method and thesis statement:** how this text and reading resolve the problem, then the thesis.
- **Body:** evidence, close reading, critical citations interwoven, one move per section with breaks between major moves.
- **Close:** restate refined thesis plus what follows for the field.

## 5. Opinion op-ed, hook-argument-close

- **Length:** 500-800 words.
- **Hook:** anecdote, striking claim, or statistic framing one central argument.
- **Body:** single line of reasoning advanced paragraph by paragraph. Acknowledge the best counter briefly, answer it, keep moving.
- **Close:** call to action or memorable restatement. No new evidence.
- **Sources:** minimal inline attribution, no bibliography unless the outlet requires it.

## 6. Citation rules

- **APA 7 author-date:** sciences, social sciences, interdisciplinary reviews. In-text `(Rawls, 1971, p. 45)`. Reference: `Rawls, J. (1971). A theory of justice. Harvard University Press.` Include DOI for journal articles.
- **Chicago Notes-Bibliography:** humanities and philosophy default. Footnote: `1. John Rawls, A Theory of Justice (Cambridge, MA: Harvard University Press, 1971), 45.` Bibliography entry accordingly.
- **Chicago author-date:** interdisciplinary option. In-text `(Rawls 1971, 45)` with reference list entry.
- **Hygiene:** every in-text citation maps to exactly one reference entry. No uncited entries except personal communications, which stay out of the list. Verify DOIs, drop retracted sources.

## 7. Peer-review self-check detail

- Relevance and originality: gap named, contribution stated.
- Clarity: same objective in title, abstract, introduction, conclusion.
- Rigor: design, sampling, procedure, analysis described for replication with the relevant checklist item cited.
- Presentation: numbers with uncertainty, figures referenced by number, no prose duplication of full tables.
- Interpretation: claims bounded by data, causal language only with causal design, limitations tied to impact.
- Ethics and form: approvals, consent, conflicts declared. Headings and references follow the target venue.
